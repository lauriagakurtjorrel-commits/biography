What I Wish I Knew Before Taking IT

Introduction

When I first decided to take Information Technology, I thought it was mostly about computers and typing fast on a keyboard. I imagined myself writing code easily and understanding everything right away. Honestly, I underestimated how challenging the course would be. I did not expect that IT would test my patience, discipline, and problem-solving skills almost every day. Looking back, there are many things I wish I had known before officially becoming an IT student. This blog is about those realizations and lessons I learned along the way.

IT Is Not Just About Coding

Before taking IT, I thought coding was the only thing I needed to learn. I was surprised to discover that IT involves many areas such as networking, databases, and system analysis. Coding is important, but it is only one part of the bigger picture. I learned that understanding concepts matters more than memorizing syntax. Sometimes, knowing why something works is more important than knowing how to write it.

Problem-Solving Over Speed

At first, I focused too much on typing speed and tools. Later on, I realized that speed does not matter if I don’t understand the problem. There were moments when I stared at my screen for hours trying to find solutions. IT trained me to think logically and break problems into smaller parts. This mindset slowly changed how I approach challenges even outside school.

Learning Never Stops

One thing I wish I knew earlier is that learning in IT does not stop after class. Many topics required self-study and practice. Watching tutorials and reading documentation became part of my routine. I realized that IT students must be independent learners. Waiting for everything to be spoon-fed only leads to frustration.

Conclusion and Reflection

Taking IT taught me more than just technical skills. It taught me patience, discipline, and persistence. Struggling became part of the learning process. If I could give advice to my past self, I would say not to be afraid of confusion. IT is challenging, but it is rewarding for those who are willing to grow.
